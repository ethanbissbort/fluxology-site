## Verdicts

- **APPLY** — no hard gate violated, realistic attainability, and clear
  T176 career value.
- **APPLY — STRATEGIC STRETCH** — a meaningful experience/qualification gap
  exists but is stated as preferred/asset (or reads as boilerplate), and the
  career value justifies the attempt. Every STRETCH needs an explicit
  stretch rationale.
- **MAYBE** — useful but carries a substantial schedule, commute, skill,
  credential, or career-value tradeoff worth the user's judgement.
- **SKIP** — a hard gate fails (mandatory licence/years/apprenticeship
  level, supervisory scope, study-incompatible schedule) or the career
  value is too weak (generic labour without tool/machinery/trade exposure).

## T176 career-fit score (0–10)

Score the role's contribution to trades career capital, weighted toward the
active semester (currently welding + plumbing):

- 9–10: direct daily exposure to an active-semester trade with training or
  an apprenticeship pathway (e.g. fabrication helper who tacks and grinds;
  mechanical-piping helper).
- 7–8: daily shop-floor exposure to tools/metal/piping adjacent to the
  active trades (machine-shop helper, metal finishing, weld cleanup).
- 5–6: real industrial exposure with partial transfer (industrial
  maintenance helper, sheet metal, HVAC helper, millwright environment).
- 3–4: construction/industrial labour with some tool or site exposure but
  little instruction.
- 0–2: generic labour/warehouse/retail with no meaningful transfer.

## Ranking order (fixed unless the user changes priorities)

1. T176 career value
2. Realistic attainability
3. School-schedule compatibility
4. Total public-transit burden
5. Last-mile difficulty (skateboard-adjusted)
6. Pay and benefits (tie-breaker only)

## Shortlist-chance estimates

Always a clearly labelled estimate, expressed as a broad range (e.g.
"~15–35%, estimate"), grounded in: stated requirements vs the user's
zero-experience/strong-motivation profile, posting age, role scarcity, and
whether the employer signals willingness to train. Never present a point
probability and never inflate to encourage.
